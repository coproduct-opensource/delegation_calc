DLC's metatheory is organized around four headline theorems.  This
section states each theorem in canonical form, reports its current
mechanization status, and points to the Lean proof file containing
the partial or complete proof.

\subsection{T1 -- Decidability of proof-checking}

\begin{theorem}[T1, propositional fragment]
For every additive context $\Gamma_a$, term $M$, and proposition
$\varphi$ in the propositional fragment of DLC, the question ``does
$M$ prove $\varphi$ in $\Gamma_a$?'' is decidable.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Status: \texttt{proven\_fragment}.}
Mechanized in \texttt{lean/DLC/Decidability.lean}: a decision function
\texttt{decideLean}, its soundness and completeness against the
syntax-directed judgment \texttt{PropDeriv} (22 constructors, additive
contexts), and the resulting \texttt{Decidable} instance --- all
sorry-free, with a non-vacuity witness
(\texttt{lean/DLC/Witness/T1.lean}) demonstrating both acceptance of a
well-typed term and rejection, through completeness, of an ill-typed
one.  Two gaps are open and tracked in the ledger: decidability for
the \emph{full} calculus (linear context splitting is the hard part
--- the propositional fragment does not exercise it), and any
complexity bound.  The $O(|M| \cdot \log |\Gamma|)$ figure that
earlier revisions of this artifact advertised is a design target,
unproven in any form, for any fragment.

The Rust function (\texttt{crates/dlc-core/src/decide.rs}) handles all
22 term constructors, including the no-chain-splicing condition on
\texttt{delegate}, but does not enforce linear context splitting.
Nine unit tests, including a negative
\texttt{delegate\_rejects\_chain\_splicing} case, document the
implementation.

\subsection{T2 -- Cryptographic correspondence}

\begin{theorem}[T2, symbolic characterization --- the proven form]
For every keyring $K$, additive context $\Gamma_a$, term $M$, and
proposition $\varphi$ in the propositional fragment:
\[
\Gamma_a \vdash_K M : \varphi
\iff
\Gamma_a \vdash M : \varphi \;\wedge\; \mathsf{allSigsVerify}_K(M)
\]
Cryptographic typing coincides with logical typing plus verification
of every signature embedded in the subject term.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Status: characterization \texttt{proven} (axiom-free);
attacker-based form \texttt{open}.}
The characterization above is mechanized in both directions in
\texttt{lean/DLC/Correspondence.lean}; \texttt{\#print axioms} reports
only \texttt{propext}.  We state its strength plainly: $\vdash_K$ is
\emph{defined} as the refinement of the syntax-directed propositional
judgment by a verification premise on \textsc{says-I}, so this is a
definitional-refinement correspondence of the same class as Aura's
(Jia et al., 2008), not a cryptographic soundness theorem.  An earlier
revision of this artifact instead postulated the unqualified iff
$\Gamma \vdash M : \varphi \iff \Gamma \vdash_K M : \varphi$ modulo an
axiom \texttt{Sig\_EUF\_CMA\_propositional}; that axiom was refutable
in-system (the theory including it proved \texttt{False}), a finding
now machine-checked as a regression test in
\texttt{lean/DLC/Witness/AxiomAudit.lean}.

The attacker-based T2 --- acceptance by the executable verifier
implies a derivation from the assumptions of principals whose keys are
uncompromised, against an explicit Dolev-Yao adversary, with
wire-encoding injectivity inside the statement and an EUF-CMA
reduction at the base --- is open.  It requires a working verifier and
a mechanized wire-format model before it can even be stated against
real artifacts; \texttt{models/easycrypt/Game.eca} documents the
intended game-hop sequence but contains no proof content (its
placeholder axiom encodes nothing).  Candidate mechanization routes
are EasyCrypt with cryptographer pairing, or VCVio (Tuma et al.,
2026)~\cite{tuma2026vcvio} within Lean.

\subsection{T3 -- Non-interference under delegation}

\begin{theorem}[T3, target statement --- open]
For every IFC label $\ell_{\mathit{low}}$ and every pair of well-typed
derivations of the same term shape that differ only in hypotheses at
labels $\not\leq \ell_{\mathit{low}}$, the two runs are
indistinguishable to an observer at $\ell_{\mathit{low}}$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Status: \texttt{stated}; no form of non-interference is
currently proven.}
We are explicit about what exists: the Lean development
(\texttt{lean/DLC/NonInterference.lean}) defines the unary-relation
infrastructure ($\mathsf{Indistinguishable}$ with reflexivity,
symmetry, transitivity), and its only theorems relate every term
\emph{to itself} with the typing derivation discarded --- one-run
reflexivity facts that hold for ill-typed terms and carry no
information-flow content.  An earlier revision of this section
presented self-relatedness as the formal content of T3; that was a
conflation, and the Lean file now disavows it in-source.  The real
theorem requires a \emph{two-run} logical relation over pairs of
derivations differing in high-labeled hypotheses and a fundamental
lemma proven by induction on the derivation
(Garg-Pfenning~CSF'06 / FLAFOL~PLDI'20 style).

iris-tini (Gregersen \& Bay, POPL
2021)~\cite{gregersen2021tini} provides the reference proof technique
in Coq+Iris.  DLC's setting is simpler (no higher-order state, no
recursive types), so a plain Lean construction without Iris should
suffice; cf.\ \texttt{spec/t2-t3-lean-feasibility-2026-05.md}.

\subsection{T4 -- Obligation soundness}

\begin{theorem}[T4, non-introduction direction]
For every reduction $M \rhd M'$ and every obligation $o$:
\[
o \in \mathsf{pendingObligations}(M') \implies o \in \mathsf{pendingObligations}(M)
\]
That is, reduction never introduces an obligation that was not
syntactically present beforehand.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Status: \texttt{stated} --- the proven direction is
vacuous.}
The non-introduction direction above \emph{is} mechanized
(\texttt{t4\_no\_new\_obligation}, a full case analysis over
$\mathsf{step}$'s redexes with a substitution-subset lemma), but we
report it as \texttt{stated}, not proven, because the statement is
currently without content: no \texttt{Term} constructor carries an
\texttt{Obligation}, so $\mathsf{pendingObligations}$ is provably the
constant empty list --- a fact we prove explicitly as
\texttt{pendingObligations\_eq\_nil} in
\texttt{lean/DLC/ObligationSoundness.lean}.  The theorem quantifies
over members of $[]$.  T4 acquires content only after the calculus
gains an obligation-carrying constructor (the \textsc{box-I} payload)
and a discharge redex in the operational semantics; the ledger's T4
witness (a term with a non-empty obligation list) is unsatisfiable
until then, and the case-analysis machinery is retained for reuse at
that point.  The full obligation-soundness statement (multiset
multiplicities, discharge consumption, and the label-ledger fusion
property) is future work on the same schedule.
