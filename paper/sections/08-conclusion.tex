% Section 8 -- Conclusion and Future Work
% Target: 1 page.

DLC unifies authorization logic, symbolic cryptography, a stated
computational-cryptography bridge, runtime wire format, and
reproducible CI in a single artifact where the same
chain-splicing-defeating rule appears at every layer.  The
unification is the contribution; the individual pieces build on
Garg-Pfenning (logic), Cremers-Blanchet (symbolic crypto), and Tuma
et al.~(VCVio, computational crypto in Lean).

\paragraph{What is proven.}  Sorry-free, with \texttt{propext} as the
only axiom (statuses machine-validated against
\texttt{lean/theorem-status.json}):
\begin{itemize}
\item T1 for the propositional fragment: soundness and completeness
  of \texttt{decideLean} against the 22-constructor syntax-directed
  judgment, with a \texttt{Decidable} instance and a non-vacuity
  witness.
\item The T2 symbolic characterization (crypto typing $\iff$ logical
  typing $\wedge$ signature validity), both directions --- plus the
  machine-checked refutation of the earlier revision's T2 axiom
  (\texttt{Witness/AxiomAudit.lean}).
\item T4's non-introduction direction as a full step-case analysis
  --- reported \texttt{stated} because it is currently vacuous
  (\texttt{pendingObligations\_eq\_nil}); the vacuity itself is a
  proven, tripwire-protected fact.
\item Three graded comonad laws; four strong indexed monad laws;
  the structural shift/substitution lemmas.
\item Three Tamarin lemmas (secrecy, non-splicing, executability) and
  three ProVerif queries (same properties, independent prover) ---
  over a deliberately small 5-rule model.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is stated but open.}  T1 full-calculus decidability
(linear splitting) and any complexity bound; the attacker-based T2
(Dolev-Yao adversary, wire injectivity, EUF-CMA reduction --- the
EasyCrypt file is a skeleton with no proof content); T3 in any form
(the current Lean lemmas are one-run reflexivity infrastructure, not
non-interference); T4's contentful form (obligation-carrying
constructor, discharge redex, multiset accounting); substitution
composition; full-\textsf{Deriv} subject reduction; the \S 4.4
protocol-logic correspondence beyond singleton traces.  Each is
tracked with its closure path in the repository's machine-validated
ledger.

\paragraph{What is not in this paper.}  We make no claim to having
established DLC as a paradigm.  That bar requires (a) full closure of
the open theorems, (b) external citation by adjacent work,
(c) standards-track adoption, (d) deployment beyond the reference
implementation.  Each of these depends on actors beyond the authors
and on multi-quarter time horizons.  We position the present paper as
a \emph{credible foundation} for that future work, with the artifact's
double-prover symbolic verification and reproducible ledger as
methodological contributions on their own.

\paragraph{Future work.}  In the medium term, four closure milestones
in priority order:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Implement the verifier and cryptographic layer end-to-end
  (Ed25519 verification, canonical bytes, COSE envelope, published
  test vectors) --- pure engineering, and the gating item for any
  claim about the wire format.
\item Real two-run T3 with a fundamental lemma
  (propositional$+$\textsf{says} fragment first), and a non-vacuous
  T4 (obligation-carrying constructor, discharge redex, multiset
  accounting).
\item T1 for linear contexts, with the complexity-bound target either
  proven or permanently retired.
\item The attacker-based T2: Aeneas-extracted checker equivalence,
  Comparse-style wire injectivity, then the symbolic-adversary
  statement, then the EUF-CMA reduction (EasyCrypt or VCVio) ---
  the last two with collaborators.
\end{enumerate}

In the longer term, collaborator engagement: Pfenning/Garg on T1/T3
proof technique, Cremers on Tamarin/ProVerif review, Blanchet on the
EUF-CMA reduction's tightness, Myers on IFC and obligation
algebra.  An IETF SECDISPATCH presentation of the wire-format draft
is the natural standards-track entry point.

\paragraph{The diagonal as the contribution.}  We close by stating
the contribution claim at its honest strength: DLC is, to our
knowledge, the first delegation calculus in which the same
chain-splicing-defeating rule appears across (a) a Lean-mechanized
type system, (b) a Tamarin symbolic model, (c) a ProVerif independent
symbolic model, (d) an EasyCrypt computational skeleton (shape only;
no proof content yet), (e) a Rust wire format with content-addressed
encoding.  The layers agree today by construction and convention;
making their agreement a machine-checked theorem is precisely the
open program laid out above.  Whether DLC ultimately earns the name
of a paradigm depends on closures and adoption beyond this paper's
horizon.  What this paper contributes is the diagonal itself, with
the mechanical evidence per axis stated at exactly the strength the
artifact's machine-validated ledger will defend.
